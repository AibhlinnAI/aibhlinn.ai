AI and automation

AibhlínnAI gives organisations practical support to identify and implement automation strategies in the work they routinely take on.

For organisations

How an AI and automation project runs

  1. Identify the time-consuming tasks

    You tell us about the tasks that take up time in your organisation. Together we choose one as a candidate for automation that is small, repeated and easy to check.

  2. Implement new time-efficient processes

    We set up the necessary structures and guardrails for that one task, either under a pilot or in some cases with immediate effect. Other work carries on as usual.

  3. Assess the new processes' effectiveness

    You see the new processes working on that task. We tell you plainly what worked and what did not, and make further recommendations to benefit your organisation.

  4. You decide

    For pilots, nothing is rolled out until you have seen the new processes in action, received our recommendations and chosen to go ahead. If the pilot did not help, we will say so.

Privacy guardrails and risk mitigation

  • Before we start a project, we will agree with you about the information that will be used.
  • We will name the services that will handle your information. Our own privacy notice does the same.
  • We will use sample data, not real records about real people, unless we have an agreement to the contrary with you in place beforehand.
  • We design each project so that a person checks each result.

What you get

  • A project on one task, which you can try for yourself.
  • A plain language explanation of what the project covers, what the project does not, and what the project needs from your people.
  • A straight answer on whether going further is worthwhile.
  • We do not make savings promises in advance. Our partnership in your project is how we find what works for your organisation.

Our work examples on our Services page are from events and from our own app.

What suits automation, and what does not

Work tasks that suit automation

Automation suits work that is repeated, follows clear rules and is easy to check, including:

  • copying data and information from one system to another
  • sorting, labelling and summarising text, with a person approving the outcome
  • first drafts of routine documents, for a person to review, edit and approve
  • reminders and routine follow-ups.

Keeping people at the centre

AI tools can give answers that are wrong for your context and still sound confident. Therefore, some work will always need a person to decide, including:

  • decisions about someone's job, health, money or rights
  • anything where a mistake is hard to notice or hard to undo
  • conversations that need care and human-centred understanding.

We will tell you when automation is the wrong tool. Sometimes a person with a checklist or a template to verify their effort and expertise does the job better.

Agents in our practice

We use AI agents in our own business

AibhlínnAI works under the same principles we bring to your organisation. AI agents do repeated work, written instructions set the limits, and the decisions that matter go to a person.

We use two kinds of AI agent:

  • Scheduled agents: these agents run unattended passive sessions, without human interaction, and prepare work designed to support a person's productivity.
  • Assistance agents: these agents work in active sessions, involving human interaction, to build with a person directing each step.

What our scheduled agents do

  • Sort incoming email and draft replies, for a person to read, edit and send.
  • Run on a schedule to prepare a checklist for the next manual job, release checks and a short summary of the human tasks to complete.
  • Record at the end of a run what was done, which sources could not be read and what is waiting on a person.

What our assistance agents do

  • Write and change code, tests and first drafts of our pages, for a person to approve.
  • Review the work of other agents. These secondary agents have one job in our reviews: to find faults.

What stays with a person

Our golden rule

Our agents produce outputs, a person decides.

Our human involvement

We read and send every reply, and approve each release before the release goes live.

Our agent guardrails

  • An agent sends nothing to another person, and does not publish, spend or delete without a person's approval.
  • No agent changes instructions without a person's approval, for themselves or another agent.
  • Each change to an agent's instructions is logged and dated.
  • These limits are written into the instructions for every scheduled agent.
  • For assistance agents the limits are set in each session's standing rules and the human approvals they receive.

What we have learned

An agent can run on time and still produce work nobody uses

Our first review checked whether an agent's work was used, and where the answer was no, the agent was given a new job. One of our agents raised the same false alarm on six mornings when an external web page changed. Our instructions contained a fault, so our instructions were rewritten.

In our practice, a fact and a theory are best kept apart

A fault's suspected cause stays labelled as a theory until a test confirms the cause. When using a generated dashboard interface, figures are updated only when an agent has read the source in that run. Anything unread is shown with the age of the reading.

Agents can only work when the lights are on

Our scheduled agents are not superhuman, and a scheduled run can be impacted due to human intervention within the scheduled agent's environment.
